# A Practical Field Guide to AI Search Visibility

AI search visibility is the ability of a brand to be correctly understood, cited, and recommended in answer engines. It is not a promise that every model will mention a company. It is a disciplined process of making a business easier to verify.

## Start with evidence

Before changing a website, collect the public facts that a buyer would need: what the company does, who it serves, where it operates, what its product costs, and what independent sources confirm those claims. An answer engine cannot confidently recommend a business when the underlying evidence is vague or contradictory.

## Test real buyer questions

Generic ranking checks are weak evidence. Write down the questions a qualified buyer would actually ask, including comparisons, constraints, location, budget, integration requirements, and risk. Test those questions consistently and save dated results. The useful signal is not one flattering answer; it is the pattern across multiple realistic questions and systems.

## Make the website legible

Clear service pages, concrete use cases, transparent limitations, structured facts, and consistent entity information help both people and machines. Avoid hidden pages made only for crawlers. Useful public documentation should remain readable, accurate, and connected to the main site.

## Build corroboration

A company's own website is only one source. Relevant profiles, directories, product documentation, reviews, case studies, and community references can corroborate important claims. Quality and consistency matter more than manufacturing a large number of weak mentions.

## Measure without overclaiming

Track answer inclusion, citation accuracy, referral traffic, qualified conversations, and revenue where available. Separate direct observations from estimates. Record the cost in tokens, money, and human time so that a visible improvement does not conceal an unmaintainable workflow.

## Run small experiments

Choose one hypothesis, one audience, a limited budget, and a clear stopping rule. Package the result for each legitimate distribution channel, measure it, then keep, improve, pause, or kill the experiment. The goal is a repeatable evidence loop, not an impressive pile of generated content.
